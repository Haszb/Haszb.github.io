## The problem

A crypto whitepaper serves two purposes: it describes a project and promotes it. It is therefore an important source of information, but does not, on its own, guarantee the quality of the project it presents.

Investors must often evaluate projects with a limited history, based on incomplete information and complex technical documents. Research on the ICO ecosystem shows that roughly half of investors consult whitepapers, while a smaller share analyse them in detail. Many lack the time or expertise to assess their contents independently.

Intermediaries do not necessarily solve the problem. Research on ICO analysts has found a bias towards positive ratings and conflicts of interest, notably when a good rating can help secure future advisory work. One study estimated that 64% of ICOs failed, and that the failure rate remained 53% among projects rated well by top-quartile experts. These are historical findings from the ICO ecosystem, not an estimate of the failure rate across today's entire crypto market.

Yet the documents contain measurable information. Research has studied features such as readability, length, language errors and tone, and how they relate to project characteristics and subsequent outcomes.

Taken alone, none of these features can determine whether a project is legitimate or predict its success. They can, however, provide observable signals, measured consistently and examined alongside other evidence.

The challenge is to move from analysing isolated documents to comparing them systematically at scale.

## The approach: measure, don't rate

Rochondra initially set out to provide a score, but that approach raised two problems.

First, without a reliable labelled dataset, assigning weights to different indicators would introduce subjective assumptions that would be difficult to validate. Second, existing research on ICO ratings has documented structural biases and conflicts of interest that make a single composite rating difficult to interpret.

The project therefore took a different direction: measure first, interpret second.

Indicators are selected from published scientific work where possible, and their calculation methods are documented. Rather than producing an aggregate score, a risk category or an investment recommendation, Rochondra reports, for each document, raw values and its position within the corresponding corpus distribution.

A whitepaper can therefore be compared with other documents on specific dimensions, without reducing all its characteristics to a single number.

This distinction is essential. The measurements describe the document, not the overall quality of the project or of the people behind it. Their relevance depends on the research behind each indicator, the quality of the underlying corpus and the limits of each measurement.

## Building the corpus

The corpus was built from CoinMarketCap, the only source found that provides whitepaper links for free across a broad collection of projects. Today, only about one whitepaper in five is still reachable on the web, so dead links were recovered from the Internet Archive with a parallel collector.

Every retrieval attempt is recorded, including failures. This makes it possible to quantify the documents that were not recovered and to measure selection bias, linked to link availability and archive coverage, as well as the truncation of some archived documents.

The corpus is not a random sample of all crypto whitepapers. It reflects CoinMarketCap's coverage, the survival of old documents and the limits of PDF extraction. These constraints must stay visible in every analysis.

It is also mostly made up of post-ICO documents, while much of the research behind the indicators studied documents from the ICO era.

An indicator validated on ICO-era whitepapers may behave differently on documents written for newer project models. Rebalancing the eras is therefore part of the research work too, not just data preparation.

## Architecture

The project was designed from day one as an application able to run the same way locally and in the cloud, rather than as a notebook to be productionised later. Logic is exposed through an API, storage is split by role, and processing is designed not to depend on files written to local disk.

<Architecture lang="en" />

The architecture separates document processing, API access and persistence. This separation aims to make components easier to test, replace and extend, without coupling the analysis logic to the interface or the storage implementation.

## Four decisions that shaped it

The [decision log](https://github.com/Haszb/Rochondra/blob/main/docs/decision-log.md) records the problems encountered, the alternatives ruled out and the trade-offs behind the main technical choices. Four decisions explain much of the project's current architecture and direction.

<Decision id="D6" title="Deterministic first, the LLM as arbiter" anchor="d6-deterministic-first-the-llm-as-arbiter" cost="Every model call needs the problem structured upstream, and generated text must stay identifiable so it can be kept out of the measurements.">
  LLMs are non-deterministic, which is incompatible with a corpus whose rows must be comparable. Measurements therefore favour proven deterministic methods. Where document structure creates ambiguity, an LLM can select between candidates produced by earlier processing steps, but can never generate an answer of its own.
</Decision>

<Decision id="D21" title="Measurements rather than ratings" anchor="d21-measurements-rather-than-ratings" cost="Harder to read at a glance, and no overall ranking, even though some users will expect one.">
  The initial goal was a fundamentals score. A review of rating platforms showed structurally optimistic assessments, documented conflicts of interest and, in several cases, sanctions. With no labelled measure of quality to test against, any weighting would also be arbitrary. Rochondra outputs raw values and positions within a distribution instead.
</Decision>

<Decision id="D24" title="Recovery through the Internet Archive" anchor="d24-recovery-through-the-internet-archive" cost="The corpus inherits the archive's biases, which are measured and reported with every analysis rather than hidden.">
  Keeping only documents still online would have produced a corpus of survivors. Querying the Internet Archive made it possible to recover a substantial part of the missing ICO-era corpus, the period hit hardest by vanished websites.
</Decision>

<Decision id="D16" title="Three storage layers, one role each" anchor="d16-three-storage-layers-one-role-each" cost="Three systems to run instead of one.">
  Redis holds the working area, MinIO stores files and analysis results, and PostgreSQL keeps durable facts in a star schema. Nothing reaches the database before finalisation, so that a submitted document can eventually be checked before anything about it is stored.
</Decision>

## Where it stands

The project has moved beyond the initial research and notebook stage. A working prototype processes PDF whitepapers through text extraction, structural measurements, table-of-contents detection and section-level sentiment analysis.

Development is organised in phases ordered by dependency rather than by calendar dates. Each phase has a verifiable exit criterion and ends with something public: an article, a site update, or both.

<Roadmap lang="en" current={1} />

Phase 1 runs the whole chain once on a small scale: 210 whitepapers, 70 per era, checked by hand, measured with structural indicators and published on the project's own site with its methodology. The full plan, card by card, is in the [roadmap](https://github.com/Haszb/Rochondra/blob/main/docs/roadmap.md).

## What comes next

The project's framework has five pillars: team, technology and code, tokenomics, documentation and community. Whitepapers belong to documentation, the first pillar to be addressed. They come first because they are public, can be processed in batches and state what a project intends to do. The longer-term aim is to compare what projects claim with what can be observed elsewhere, once the document methodology has been validated.

Price prediction, trading signals and investment recommendations are outside the project's scope.

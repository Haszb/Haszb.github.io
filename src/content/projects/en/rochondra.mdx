## The problem

A crypto whitepaper serves two purposes: it describes a project and promotes it. This dual role makes it an important source of information, but not an independent guarantee of quality.

Investors must often evaluate projects with limited operating history, incomplete information and substantial technical complexity. Research on the ICO ecosystem has found that roughly half of investors consult whitepapers, while a smaller proportion analyse them in detail. Many lack the time or expertise to assess their contents independently.

Intermediaries do not necessarily solve the problem. Research on ICO analysts has documented optimistic rating patterns and conflicts of interest, including incentives to secure future advisory work. One study reported that 64% of ICOs failed, including 53% of projects that received positive ratings from top-quartile experts. These are historical findings from the ICO ecosystem, not estimates of failure rates across today's entire crypto market.

Yet the documents themselves contain measurable information. Research has examined features such as readability, length, language errors and narrative tone in relation to project characteristics and subsequent outcomes.

None of these features can establish whether a project is legitimate or predict its success on its own. Their value lies in providing observable signals that can be measured consistently and examined alongside other evidence.

The challenge is to move from isolated document analysis to systematic comparison across a large corpus.

## The approach: measure, don't rate

Rochondra began with the idea of building a fundamentals score. That approach raised two problems.

First, without a reliable labelled dataset, assigning weights to different indicators would introduce subjective assumptions that would be difficult to validate. Second, existing research on ICO ratings has documented structural biases and conflicts of interest that make a single composite rating difficult to interpret.

The project therefore took a different direction: measure first, interpret second.

Indicators are selected from published research where possible, and their calculation methods are documented. Rather than producing an aggregate score, risk category or investment recommendation, Rochondra reports raw measurements and each document's position within the corresponding corpus distribution.

A whitepaper can therefore be compared with other documents on specific dimensions without collapsing those differences into a single number.

This distinction matters. The measurements describe the document, not the overall quality of the project or the people behind it. Their relevance depends on the research supporting each indicator, the quality of the underlying corpus and the limits of the measurement itself.

## Building the corpus

The corpus was built from CoinMarketCap, the only source found that provides whitepaper links for free across a broad collection of projects. On the live web, only about one whitepaper in five is still reachable, so dead links were recovered from the Internet Archive with a parallel collector.

Every attempt is recorded, including failed retrievals. This makes it possible to quantify the documents that were not recovered and to measure the biases linked to link availability and archive coverage.

The corpus is not a random sample of all crypto whitepapers. It reflects CoinMarketCap's coverage, the survival of old documents and the limits of PDF extraction, and these constraints stay visible in every analysis.

It is also mostly post-ICO, while most of the research behind the indicators was based on ICO-era documents. An indicator validated on 2018 whitepapers may not behave the same way on documents written for newer project models, so the balance between eras is part of the research, not just data preparation.

## Architecture

The project was designed from day one as an application that runs the same way locally and in the cloud, not as a notebook to be productionised later. Logic is exposed through an API, storage is split by role, and nothing is written to local disk.

<Architecture lang="en" />

The architecture separates document processing, API access and persistence. This makes individual components easier to test, replace and extend without coupling the analysis logic to the interface or storage implementation.

## Four decisions that shaped it

The [decision log](https://github.com/Haszb/Rochondra/blob/main/docs/decision-log.md) records the problems, alternatives and trade-offs behind the project's main technical choices. Four decisions explain much of its current direction.

<Decision id="D6" title="Deterministic first, the LLM as arbiter" anchor="d6-deterministic-first-the-llm-as-arbiter" cost="Every model call needs the problem structured upstream, and generated text must stay identifiable so it can be kept out of the measurements.">
  LLMs are non-deterministic, which is incompatible with a corpus whose rows must be comparable. Measurements therefore favour proven deterministic methods. Where document structure creates ambiguity, an LLM can select between candidates produced by earlier processing steps, but can never generate an answer of its own.
</Decision>

<Decision id="D21" title="Measurements rather than ratings" anchor="d21-measurements-rather-than-ratings" cost="Harder to read at a glance, and no overall ranking, even though some users will expect one.">
  The initial goal was a fundamentals score. A review of rating platforms showed structurally optimistic assessments, documented conflicts of interest and, in several cases, sanctions. With no labelled measure of quality to test against, any weighting would also be arbitrary. Rochondra outputs raw values and positions within a distribution instead.
</Decision>

<Decision id="D24" title="Recovery through the Internet Archive" anchor="d24-recovery-through-the-internet-archive" cost="The corpus inherits the archive's biases, which are measured and reported with every analysis rather than hidden.">
  Keeping only documents still online would have produced a corpus of survivors. Querying the Internet Archive made it possible to recover a substantial part of the missing ICO-era corpus, the period hit hardest by vanished websites.
</Decision>

<Decision id="D16" title="Three storage layers, one role each" anchor="d16-three-storage-layers-one-role-each" cost="Three systems to run instead of one.">
  Redis holds the working area, MinIO stores files and analysis results, and PostgreSQL keeps durable facts in a star schema. Nothing reaches the database before finalisation, so that a submitted document can eventually be checked before anything about it is stored.
</Decision>

## Where it stands

The project has moved beyond the initial research and notebook stage. A working prototype processes PDF whitepapers through text extraction, structural measurements, table-of-contents detection and section-level sentiment analysis.

Development is organised in phases ordered by dependency rather than by calendar dates. Each phase has a verifiable exit criterion and ends with something public: an article, a site update, or both.

<Roadmap lang="en" current={1} />

Phase 1 runs the whole chain once on a small scale: 210 whitepapers, 70 per era, checked by hand, measured with structural indicators and published on the project's own site with its methodology. The full plan, card by card, is in the [roadmap](https://github.com/Haszb/Rochondra/blob/main/docs/roadmap.md).

## What comes next

The project's framework has five pillars: team, technology and code, tokenomics, documentation and community. Whitepapers belong to documentation, the first pillar to be addressed. They come first because they are public, can be processed in batches and state what a project intends to do. The longer-term aim is to compare what projects claim with what can be observed elsewhere, once the document methodology has been validated.

Price prediction, trading signals and investment recommendations are outside the project's scope.
